1. Why we need a new view of water use

The problem is when, where, and how clean water is.

Water saving is often about pipes, pumps, and canals. Nature Based Solutions add a bigger view: use land, plants, and soil to hold rain, refill groundwater, and keep water cleaner.

  • People now take about three times more water than 50 years ago.
  • A growing population needs much more fresh water every year.
  • By 2030, almost half of people may live in places with water stress.

2. What counts as NbS?

Nature is not decoration. It is useful infrastructure.

Nature Based Solutions use healthy natural systems to solve human problems. They protect or restore places like rivers, wetlands, soils, forests, and farms.

  • More useful water: refill groundwater and keep rivers flowing.
  • Cleaner water: trap soil, fertilizer, chemicals, and waste.
  • Less risk: reduce floods, drought damage, erosion, and heat.
  • Extra benefits: more habitat, cooler cities, recreation, and health.
3. How NbS improves water efficiency

Efficiency means making water more useful.

The main idea is to change what happens to rain: instead of rushing away dirty and fast, water can slow down, soak into the ground, be cleaned, and be used later.

  • Plants and roots help water enter the soil.
  • Wetlands, small channels, ponds, and floodplains slow stormwater.
  • Soil, plants, and tiny organisms clean water naturally.
  • Some areas help refill groundwater for dry periods.

4. Where solutions operate

A river area works like one connected system.

The same storm can become a flood, stored water, or clean river flow depending on the land it passes through.

Hills and slopesplants and soil cover reduce fast runoff and erosion.
Rivers and wetlandsriver bends, buffers, and flood areas slow water.
Farmscover crops and low water crops reduce irrigation needs.
Citiesgreen roofs, swales, rain gardens, and ponds manage stormwater.

6. The risk landscape by 2050

Climate change makes water problems bigger.

Future water risks affect food, people, and nature at the same time. These numbers show why Nature Based Solutions need to grow.

More droughts

About 25% of land may face much more frequent drought.

Less river water

About 36% of land may not have enough surface water.

Less groundwater

Groundwater stress may increase by 30% by 2050.

More pollution

Too much nitrogen and phosphorus can harm rivers and lakes.

Nature at risk

Many important freshwater habitats may face water stress.

Food quality risk

Water stress can also reduce important nutrients in crops.

7. Farming uses the most water

Water security and food security are connected.

Irrigation helps grow food, but it can also use a lot of water. Better farming needs healthier soil, smarter irrigation, and landscapes that keep rivers and groundwater working.

8. Four landscape types

Choose the solution for the local problem.

Not every place needs the same solution. The best choice depends on the main water problem and the type of farming or city nearby.

River water stress

Mixed farming areas

Low river flow, drought, and competition between cities and farms.

  • Low water use crops
  • Better irrigation
  • River side plant buffers
  • Reconnect rivers to floodplains
Groundwater stress

Irrigated farming areas

Groundwater is used faster than it is refilled.

  • More crop variety
  • Ground cover
  • Refill groundwater on purpose
  • Restore wetlands
Water quality

High input farming areas

Fertilizer, soil, and farm chemicals can wash into water.

  • Use fertilizer more carefully
  • Keep soil covered
  • Plant filter strips and wetlands
  • Restore shellfish where suitable
Flooding

Flood prone farming areas

Some croplands flood often, but floodplains can also support nature.

  • Reconnect floodplains
  • River side plant buffers
  • Trees mixed with crops
  • Manage dams and lakes better
9. NbS for water quantity

Drought is managed from farms to rivers.

River bends and floodplains slow water and help refill soil and groundwater.
Hybrid systems combine built structures with natural water storage.

On farms

Use less irrigation and help soil hold more water.

  • Low water crops and more crop variety
  • Mulch, cover crops, and plant leftovers on soil
  • Disturb the soil less
  • Drip irrigation and careful watering
  • Better grazing and grassland recovery

Across the landscape

Refill groundwater and keep rivers flowing in dry times.

  • Protect and restore wetlands
  • River side plant buffers
  • Reconnect rivers to floodplains
  • Hold rainwater in natural areas
  • Refill groundwater on purpose
10. NbS for water quality

It is easier to stop pollution before it reaches the river.

Water gets dirty when bare soil, fertilizer, and chemicals wash away with fast runoff. Nature Based Solutions slow the water and clean it step by step.

  • On farms: careful fertilizer use, cover crops, mixed crops, less soil disturbance.
  • Across landscapes: plant strips, river side buffers, wetlands, floodplains.
  • In water bodies: shellfish and underwater plants can help clean water.

11. NbS for flood risk

Floodplains give water room to spread safely.

When rivers can spread into floodplains during storms, pressure downstream is lower. These areas can also support farms, wildlife, and groundwater refill.

24%

of global croplands are in areas that can flood

  • Floodplains slow water and refill groundwater.
  • River side plants reduce erosion and create habitat.
  • Trees and ground cover protect soil.
  • Dams and lakes can be managed together with floodplains.
12. Urban water management

Cities can act more like sponges.

Instead of sending rain away quickly, cities can hold it, let it soak into the ground, clean it, and reuse it. This can reduce pressure on drains and treatment plants.

13. Good management matters

Nature Based Solutions need care over time.

Living systems change. They need planning, checking, local discussion, maintenance, and updates when conditions change.

Planning

Clear actions, smart use of resources, long term thinking.

Monitoring

Check nature, water quality, and results, then share reports.

Consultation

Listen to local people and make decisions together.

Planning, checking, and public discussion help Nature Based Solutions work.
14. How to put it into practice

Move from small projects to a stronger landscape.

A Nature Based Solutions program should understand the problem, choose the right solution, work with local people, find funding, and keep improving.

Understand the area

Map drought, low river water, low groundwater, pollution, and flood risk.

Choose the right type

Match the solution to the main water problem.

Design with local people

Bring farmers, cities, communities, engineers, and nature experts together.

Find funding

Use public support, grants, water funds, and risk reduction budgets.

Check and improve

Track water, nature, maintenance needs, public support, and cost.

15. Limits and design care

Nature Based Solutions help, but they cannot do everything.

They work best when they match the local place. Often they also need pipes, dams, rules, maintenance, and monitoring.

Questions before starting

  • Can it handle normal storms, drought, heavy rain, or all of them?
  • Should it work at street, farm, city, groundwater, or river area scale?
  • Who will maintain plants, soils, and water paths over time?
  • How will we balance water use, farming, nature, land, and fairness?
  • Which built structures are still needed for safety?

Efficient water use

use less water + slow runoff + store water + clean pollution + refill groundwater + manage fairly
16. Final message

Work with the water cycle, not against it.

  • Plan water use for the whole river area, not only one pipe or field.
  • Good projects give more than one benefit: water supply, less risk, cleaner water, nature, and community value.
  • To grow these solutions, we need rules, money, proof, checking, and local teamwork.
